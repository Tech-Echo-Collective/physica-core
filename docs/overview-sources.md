# Overview subject expansion

The v0.5.0 additions cover calculus, linear algebra, quantum states and measurement, Lagrangian mechanics, variational methods, relativity, metric operations, coordinate connections, geodesics, and flat/Schwarzschild spacetime. Definitions are scoped introductions rather than exhaustive classifications.

Prerequisite edges are reviewed teaching choices informed by the sources, not a source-published ontology. `generalizes` is reserved for a genuine restricted case or added structure. Product grouping and subspace containment remain outside Core.

Existing definitions and aliases are preserved. In particular, `affine-connection` already owns the alias “Covariant derivative”; no duplicate canonical record was added. The existing Schwarzschild record retains its exterior scope; horizons and the maximal extension are discussed explicitly in separate concepts.

## Calculus, linear algebra and quantum mechanics

- [MIT 18.01, Lecture 2: Limits and Continuity](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01-single-variable-calculus-fall-2006/acebd5cc8fe0315270d486685739d08f_lec2.pdf): Limits, continuity, and difference-quotient derivatives; definitions paraphrased. Requires relations are editorial prerequisites.
- [MIT 18.01, Lectures 18–25: Integration](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01-single-variable-calculus-fall-2006/04b5c4f79b8f8fd8ab1a1af71e46b752_unit3_who_sept24.pdf): Riemann sums and both directions of the fundamental theorem, with continuity hypotheses.
- [MIT 18.01, Lecture 37: Taylor Series](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01-single-variable-calculus-fall-2006/f76ca4e44b2c5b208dd6f785ee1db93e_lec37.pdf): Derivative-based coefficients, power series and local approximation. Smoothness alone does not guarantee representation by the Taylor series.
- [MIT 18.02SC, Mattuck: Partial Derivatives](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/3299372c0b77500ada4ea558fea4db80_MIT18_02SC_MNotes_ta1.pdf): Fixing the other coordinates reduces the definition to an ordinary derivative. No false total-derivative generalization is asserted.
- [MIT 18.02SC, Mattuck: Plane Vector Fields](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/1a6e16c878eb5aa8b580fe021dfa7f3d_MIT18_02SC_MNotes_v1.pdf): Gradient components, inner-product directional interpretation, and level sets.
- [MIT 18.02SC: Definition of Double Integration](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/6e4a3ff68be72d26d3c70d8ea7347331_MIT18_02SC_notes_23.pdf): Multivariable accumulation via area-weighted Riemann sums; repeated integration needs applicable hypotheses.
- [MIT 18.06SC: Vector Spaces](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/pages/ax-b-and-the-four-subspaces/transposes-permutations-vector-spaces/): Vector-space structure and closure under linear combinations.
- [MIT 18.06SC: Independence, Basis and Dimension](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/0bbc30e3f1d7933ea07a2d2e9ab050d9_MIT18_06SCF11_Ses1.9sum.pdf): Linear independence and spanning. The canonical basis record explicitly uses the algebraic/Hamel convention.
- [MIT 18.06SC: Linear Transformations and their Matrices](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/cc829145eee5220b2024424bcb14c263_MIT18_06SCF11_Ses3.6sum.pdf): Linearity and coordinate matrix representation. Matrices are representations, not subtypes of maps.
- [MIT 18.06SC: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06sc-linear-algebra-fall-2011/1999c9f4accdbef05571a1014438f8dd_MIT18_06SCF11_Ses2.8sum.pdf): Nonzero eigenvector condition and operator scaling equation.
- [MIT 18.102, Melrose: Hilbert Spaces](https://math.mit.edu/~rbm/18-102-S17/Chapter3.pdf): Pre-Hilbert inner-product structure, positive definiteness and norm completeness. Refinement edges mean added structure, not pedagogical difficulty.
- [MIT, Guillemin and Sternberg: Semiclassical Analysis, §13.2](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.157/S2012/semistart.pdf): Densely defined self-adjoint operators require equality with their adjoints in domain as well as action. The linear-operator record allows specified domains.
- [MIT 8.06, Harrow: Entanglement, Density Matrices and Decoherence](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-06-quantum-physics-iii-spring-2016/fc8347d83390a876ee665fbbfd9b2d7f_MIT8_06S16_chap3.pdf): Density operators, pure and mixed states, and reduced states; ensemble decompositions are not unique.
- [Caltech, Preskill: Foundations I, States and Ensembles](https://www.preskill.caltech.edu/ph219/chap2_15.pdf): Quantum states, projective measurements, density-operator probabilities and bipartite pure-state factorization.
- [Caltech, Preskill: Quantum Shannon Theory, §10.5](https://www.preskill.caltech.edu/ph219/chap10_6A_2025.pdf): Finite-dimensional mixed-state separability as a convex combination of product states; used to scope the entanglement definition.
- [MIT 8.05, Zwiebach: Wave Mechanics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/61bc31b8d8bf0680c322733910a71aa0_MIT8_05F13_Chap_01.pdf): Complex wavefunctions, phase equivalence, Schrödinger dynamics and linear superposition.
- [MIT 22.02: Time Evolution in Quantum Mechanics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/22-02-introduction-to-applied-nuclear-physics-spring-2012/b5106a499ae03e36b5a2e002355668f9_MIT22_02S12_lec_ch6.pdf): State-vector Schrödinger evolution and superposed energy components.
- [Oxford, Joyce: Orientations and Integration](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/joyce/Nairobi2019/IDG5%2B6handout.pdf): Stokes on an oriented interval recovers the fundamental theorem of calculus.
- [Toronto, Gualtieri: Vector Bundles and Differential Forms](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/mgualt/courses/18-1300/docs/18-1300-notes-vb-dr.pdf): Tangent/cotangent linear structure, partial derivatives in exterior differentiation and coordinate integration links.

## Mechanics and variational methods

- [Iñaki García Etxebarria, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Mechanics, Durham University](https://maths.dur.ac.uk/users/inaki.garcia-etxebarria/MPII/Lectures.html): Sections 2.2, 2.3 and 4.2 support configuration space, generalized coordinates, mechanical Lagrangians and Lagrangian energy. Energy conservation here is an on-solution statement when explicit time dependence is absent.
- [David Tong, Classical Dynamics, Chapter 4, University of Cambridge](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/dynamics/four.pdf): Sections 4.1 and 4.1.1 support conjugate momentum, the local Legendre transform and Hamiltonian. Invertibility is required; singular Lagrangians need a constrained treatment.
- [José Figueroa-O'Farrill, Brief Notes on the Calculus of Variations, University of Edinburgh](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~jmf/Teaching/Lectures/CoV.pdf): Sections 1, 6, 9 and 11 support functionals, action, Noether's first theorem and multiplier use. Noether's symmetry is a variational symmetry; this is not the second theorem for gauge identities.
- [Riccardo Cristoferi, Calculus of Variations lecture notes, Carnegie Mellon University](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~rcristof/pdf/Teaching/Spring2016/Cristoferi-Calculus_of_Variations-Lecture%20notes.pdf): Lemma 3.11 and Theorem 3.7 support the compact-support fundamental lemma and classical Euler-Lagrange equation; section 4.5 discusses position constraints and multiplier functions.
- [Daniel Liberzon, First Variation, University of Illinois](https://liberzon.csl.illinois.edu/teaching/cvoc/node15.html): The first variation is a directional derivative of a differentiable functional on admissible perturbations; its vanishing is necessary for an interior extremum but does not guarantee a minimum.
- [Daniel Liberzon, Second Variation, University of Illinois](https://liberzon.csl.illinois.edu/teaching/cvoc/node16.html): Second variation is quadratic. Nonnegativity is a necessary minimum test; positive values in every direction alone need not suffice in infinite-dimensional spaces.
- [Daniel Liberzon, Variable-Endpoint Problems, University of Illinois](https://liberzon.csl.illinois.edu/teaching/cvoc/node32.html): Free boundary values enlarge admissible variations, yielding boundary stationarity conditions. In the simplest fixed-time problem without endpoint cost, a free terminal value gives partial L / partial qdot = 0 there.
- [Daniel Liberzon, Lagrange Multipliers, University of Illinois](https://liberzon.csl.illinois.edu/teaching/cvoc/node11.html): Equality-constrained first-order conditions require regular constraint gradients; multiplier equations are necessary conditions, not an unconditional existence or minimization guarantee.
- [David Tong, The Lagrangian Formalism, University of Cambridge](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/dynamics/dynhtml/S2.html): Search-indexed chapter material supports cyclic coordinates, conjugate-momentum conservation and holonomic constraints. Direct chapter fetch was unavailable during this check; independent university notes corroborate the definitions.

## Relativity and geometric structures

- [Sean Carroll — Special Relativity and Flat Spacetime](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll1.html): University-hosted primary lecture notes. Supports Minkowski metric, invariant intervals, Lorentz transformations, light cones and four-vector refinements. Use mostly-plus signature explicitly; four-velocity requires a timelike worldline.
- [David Morin — Special Relativity, Chapter 1: Kinematics](https://davidmorin.physics.fas.harvard.edu/resource/relativity-chapter-1): Harvard primary course material. Supports inertial frames, simultaneity, kinematic time dilation and simultaneity-qualified length contraction.
- [Sean Carroll — Manifolds](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll2.html): University-hosted primary lecture notes. Supports coordinate bases, metric signature, inverse metrics and metric volume elements. A global metric volume form also requires orientation.
- [Sean Carroll — Curvature](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll3.html): Primary lecture notes. Supports connection coefficients, their nontensorial coordinate transformation, covariant derivatives, compatibility and affine parameters. Null geodesics have affine parameters but no proper-time parametrization.
- [David Tong — Introducing Riemannian Geometry](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S3.html): Cambridge primary lecture notes, indexed text verified when direct fetch timed out. Normal coordinates cancel the Levi-Civita coefficients at one point, not generally curvature. Metric compatibility is separate from the additional torsion-free condition.
- [Scott Hughes — MIT 8.962 General Relativity Lecture Guide](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-962-general-relativity-spring-2020/743631236d2866f6ba129f06110d8b1a_MIT8_962S20_guide.pdf): MIT primary course notes. Supports metric volume element, free-fall equations, proper time, four-momentum and geodesic deviation. No unrestricted timelike/null/spacelike subclass chain is proposed for the general geodesic concept.
- [Sean Carroll — Gravitation](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll4.html): Primary lecture notes. Supports the local equivalence principle, Einstein equations and stress-energy as the matter source. Einstein's equations do not imply that gravitational energy has a local stress-energy tensor of the same kind.
- [David Tong — The Einstein Equations](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S4.html): Cambridge primary lecture notes, indexed text verified. Supports the Einstein tensor, cosmological term and covariant matter conservation.
- [David Tong — Geodesics in Spacetime](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S1.html): Cambridge primary lecture notes and indexed section outline. Proper-time and gravitational clock comparisons are worldline-dependent; static Schwarzschild clock comparison applies outside the horizon.
- [Sean Carroll — The Schwarzschild Solution and Black Holes](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll7.html): University-hosted primary lecture notes. Supports Kruskal extension and distinction between horizon and r = 0 singularity. The notes contain an obvious isolated typo naming the real singularity r = 2GM; surrounding derivation and the independent Tong source correctly identify r = 0.
- [David Tong — Black Holes](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S6.html): Cambridge primary lecture notes, indexed text verified. Schwarzschild outside a spherical star need not contain a black-hole horizon. Root preserves the existing exterior-focused Schwarzschild identity in this additive release; the Horizons & extension group and the Kruskal record explicitly discuss the maximal analytic extension separately. Do not assert that every exterior region contains a physical horizon.
