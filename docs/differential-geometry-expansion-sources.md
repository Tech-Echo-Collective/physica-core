# Differential geometry expansion — 0.15.1

## Boundary

This release adds a finite, teachable canonical spine; it does not copy the
Wikipedia category tree. English Wikipedia supplied current article identities
for discovery. Definitions, relation directions, distinctions, and theorem
hypotheses were checked independently. Article bodies are not stored.

Lists, biographies, journals, historical surveys, disambiguation pages, named
example catalogues, and advanced topics without a present physics or prerequisite
path remain outside Core. Illuminatio may expose those pages as reading links
without promoting them to canonical concepts.

## Academic structure and definitions

- Marco Gualtieri, University of Toronto, [Geometry and Topology I](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/mgualt/courses/MAT1300F-2016/docs/1300-2016-notes.pdf): smooth manifolds and maps, tangent bundles, rank, Sard and transversality, partitions of unity, vector fields, bundles, and differential forms.
- Columbia University, [Modern Geometry core syllabus](https://www.math.columbia.edu/courses-math/graduate-core-courses/modern-geometry/): the progression from manifolds and bundles through Riemannian geometry, global theorems, principal bundles, connections, holonomy, and characteristic classes.
- Werner Ballmann, MPI Bonn, [Differential-geometry lecture notes](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/hwbllmnn/notes.html): connections, geodesics, semi-Riemannian metrics, immersions and submersions, global Riemannian geometry, and Kähler geometry.
- Michael Thaddeus, Columbia University, [Modern Geometry II syllabus](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~thaddeus/geometry/springsyllabus.pdf): curvature, second fundamental form, Jacobi fields, Gauss–Bonnet, Hopf–Rinow, and bundle curvature.
- Ana Cannas da Silva, [Lectures on Symplectic Geometry](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.157/2017sp/lecturesonsymplecticgeometryanacannas.pdf): symplectic manifolds, Hamiltonian vector fields, momentum maps, Darboux coordinates, Poisson geometry, and the contact interface.
- Eleny Ionel, Stanford University, [Geometry and Topology of Complex Manifolds](https://math.stanford.edu/~ionel/Math270-w25.html): complex, Hermitian, and Kähler structures, connections and curvature, Hodge theory, and physics-facing geometric applications.
- Nick Sheridan, Princeton University, [Hamilton's Ricci Flow](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nsher/ricciflow.pdf): the Ricci-flow equation, curvature evolution, and its required Riemannian/PDE foundations.

## Scientific distinctions retained

- An affine connection on the tangent bundle and a general vector-bundle
  connection are related but not identical. `Covariant derivative` remains an
  alias of the established affine-connection concept in this ontology.
- Connection torsion is not the torsion of a space curve.
- An almost-complex structure need not be integrable; a complex manifold is a
  specialization.
- A Poisson manifold need not be symplectic; a symplectic form induces a
  nondegenerate Poisson structure.
- A Kähler structure simultaneously satisfies compatible complex, Hermitian,
  Riemannian, and symplectic conditions.
- Hopf–Rinow and Gauss–Bonnet are stated with their finite-dimensional,
  connectedness, compactness, and orientation assumptions in the concept text or
  consumer formula annotation rather than as unconditional slogans.
