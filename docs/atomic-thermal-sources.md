## Identity and scope boundaries

Quantum angular momentum is not classical orbital angular momentum. Magnetic
quantum numbers and intrinsic spin are general quantum concepts, not properties
owned exclusively by atomic physics. Orbital labels and electron configuration
are not classical particle trajectories. The hydrogenic model is a one-electron
Coulomb system; Bohr quantization is explicitly historical. The Stark perturbation
operator is linear in field, but the energy shift need not be. The displayed
Zeeman equation intentionally neglects spin.

An ideal gas is a model; its equation of state is a separate concept. Maxwell's
speed density includes the three-dimensional velocity-space factor and is not
just the existing Boltzmann state distribution. Equipartition has classical
quadratic-term assumptions. Mean free path's present scope is molecular gas
collisions, with transport shared into the existing viscosity concept.

Heat is energy transferred owing to temperature difference, not stored energy.
Processes are siblings, not a dependency chain. Adiabatic does not mean reversible;
isochoric eliminates boundary work, not every work mode. Heat-engine and Carnot
formulas distinguish general cyclic efficiency from the reversible two-reservoir
bound. The sign convention is ΔU = Q − W, work positive outward.

## Concept definitions and independent references

- **Atomic Physics** (`atomic-physics`): Electronic structure and dynamics of atoms and ions, including spectroscopy and interactions with radiation and applied fields. Excludes: Nuclear structure and nuclear reactions are separate subjects; this field is not synonymous with nuclear physics or the entire atomic, molecular and optical umbrella. [Reference](https://users.physics.ox.ac.uk/~Steane/teaching/al.pdf).
- **Hydrogen-like Atom** (`hydrogen-like-atom`): True one-electron atoms and ions; the representative formula treats a point nucleus and an electron through their relative coordinate. Excludes: Multi-electron alkali atoms described by effective one-electron models, exotic atom analogues, and molecular ions are not identified with this two-particle atomic system. [Reference](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-06-quantum-physics-iii-spring-2018/439fc15f7ed10dd76c69dc3f7fea600e_MIT8_06S18ch2.pdf).
- **Atomic Orbital** (`atomic-orbital`): Spatial one-electron states used to describe atomic electronic structure, including hydrogenic eigenfunctions and approximate many-electron orbitals. Excludes: Not a classical electron trajectory, an isodensity surface alone, a spin state, or the full correlated many-electron wavefunction. [Reference](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qm/qmhtml/S4.html).
- **Principal Quantum Number** (`principal-quantum-number`): The atomic shell label n and its role in hydrogenic energy levels and radial eigenfunctions. Excludes: Not the orbital angular momentum number, an arbitrary count of all energy eigenstates, or a complete energy specification for a many-electron atom. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/6-3-development-of-quantum-theory).
- **Azimuthal Quantum Number** (`azimuthal-quantum-number`): Orbital angular momentum quantum number ell, especially its use in atomic subshell labels and angular eigenfunctions. Excludes: Not the magnetic projection number, the principal shell number, or the classical magnitude of orbital angular momentum. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/8-1-the-hydrogen-atom).
- **Magnetic Quantum Number** (`magnetic-quantum-number`): Projection labels for orbital, intrinsic-spin and total angular momentum; m is defined relative to a chosen quantization axis. Excludes: Not restricted to orbital m_l, not a magnetic-field strength, and not a specification of all three angular momentum components. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qm/Quantum/node60.html).
- **Quantum Angular Momentum** (`quantum-angular-momentum`): Quantum angular momentum observables, their component algebra and quantized spectra, including orbital, spin and total forms. Excludes: Distinct from Core's classical angular-momentum identity. The quantum observable is not interchangeable with a definite classical vector or exclusively with orbital motion. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qm/Quantum/node39.html).
- **Bohr Model** (`bohr-model`): Bohr's historical quantized-orbit atomic model, used here for its one-electron Coulomb application and limitations. Excludes: Not a modern electron trajectory prediction, the Schrödinger model, or a generally accurate description of multi-electron atoms. [Reference](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qm/qmhtml/S4.html).
- **Rydberg Formula** (`rydberg-formula`): The standard hydrogen Rydberg wavelength relation; nuclear-charge generalizations and many-electron quantum-defect variants remain contextual extensions. Excludes: Not the Rydberg constant itself, a spectral-line phenomenon, or a guarantee that every transition is allowed; many-electron quantum-defect variants require separate treatment. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qmech/lectures/node83.html).
- **Spectral Line** (`spectral-line`): Narrow emission and absorption features in radiation spectra, with atomic spectroscopy as the local application. Excludes: Not a monochromatic wave by definition, an entire spectral series, or the Rydberg equation; finite line widths and non-atomic sources are compatible with the general concept. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/6-4-bohrs-model-of-the-hydrogen-atom).
- **Zeeman Effect** (`zeeman-effect`): Magnetic-field-induced energy shifts and the resulting spectral splitting; the displayed formula is the spin-neglected orbital case. Excludes: Not the Stark effect or a synonym for a particular Landé-factor formula. Strong-field mixing and nuclear contributions require additional modeling. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qm/Quantum/node72.html).
- **Stark Effect** (`stark-effect`): Electric-field-induced changes of atomic or molecular levels and spectral lines, with static uniform fields as the introductory case. Excludes: Not the Zeeman effect. AC Stark shifts, ionization and plasma line-shape modeling extend beyond the displayed static single-electron interaction. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qm/Quantum/node68.html).
- **Spin Angular Momentum** (`spin-angular-momentum`): Intrinsic angular momentum of elementary and composite particles, with the nonrelativistic spin algebra and electron spin as an example. Excludes: Not a literal rotating charged sphere, orbital angular momentum, or only electron spin. The relativistic origin and spin–statistics theorem are not asserted as consequences of the displayed algebra. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/qm/Quantum/node48.html).
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle** (`pauli-exclusion-principle`): Exclusion for identical fermions, with electron orbital occupations as the local application. Excludes: Not a prohibition against sharing a spatial orbital, not a rule for bosons, and not a derivation of the relativistic spin–statistics theorem. [Reference](https://users.physics.ox.ac.uk/~Steane/teaching/al.pdf).
- **Electron Configuration** (`electron-configuration`): Orbital occupation descriptions, with atomic shell and subshell notation as the local emphasis. Excludes: Not a full correlated many-electron state, a unique energy eigenstate, or a universal rule that all shells fill in increasing principal number. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/6-4-electronic-structure-of-atoms-electron-configurations).
- **Kinetic Theory of Gases** (`kinetic-theory-of-gases`): Classical kinetic description of gases, including dilute equilibrium models and introductory molecular transport. Excludes: Not synonymous with statistical mechanics as a whole; relativistic, quantum-degenerate, and dense-fluid extensions are outside this introductory scope. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-2-pressure-temperature-and-rms-speed).
- **Ideal Gas** (`ideal-gas`): The classical ideal-gas model, allowing molecular internal degrees of freedom while neglecting intermolecular potential energy. Excludes: The ideal-gas law is a separate equation node. Perfect gas is not an alias because some texts additionally require constant heat capacities. Quantum ideal gases are excluded. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-2-pressure-temperature-and-rms-speed).
- **Ideal Gas Law** (`ideal-gas-law`): The classical ideal-gas equation of state for a gas of specified amount in equilibrium. Excludes: Not the ideal-gas model itself, a general real-gas law, or an assumption that heat capacities are constant. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-1-molecular-model-of-an-ideal-gas).
- **Maxwell–Boltzmann Speed Distribution** (`maxwell-boltzmann-speed-distribution`): The normalized distribution of the nonnegative speed magnitude for one molecular species in a classical equilibrium gas. Excludes: Not the distribution of one velocity component, the vector velocity density, Maxwell–Boltzmann occupancy statistics, or the general Boltzmann distribution over microstates. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-4-distribution-of-molecular-speeds).
- **Equipartition Theorem** (`equipartition-theorem`): The classical quadratic-energy form of equipartition and its use in energy and heat-capacity estimates. Excludes: Does not assign equal energies to arbitrary nonquadratic terms or to quantum modes whose level spacing is thermally inaccessible. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node238.html).
- **Mean Free Path** (`mean-free-path`): The collision mean free path of molecules in a dilute gas; the displayed estimate uses identical hard spheres. Excludes: Not the momentum-relaxation transport mean free path or a photon absorption length. Molecular diameter enters a dilute collision model, not a dense-gas excluded-volume correction. [Reference](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node219.html).
- **Thermodynamic Process** (`thermodynamic-process`): Classical macroscopic changes and cycles, with closed-system examples. Quasi-static paths admit a succession of equilibrium states. Excludes: Not necessarily reversible or quasi-static. Flow-process balances require additional matter and energy accounting. [Reference](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node11.html).
- **Heat** (`heat`): Heat as a boundary energy-transfer mode, positive when entering the selected system. Excludes: Not internal energy, temperature, or mechanical or electrical work. Identifying the system boundary is part of classifying the energy transfer. [Reference](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node12.html).
- **Heat Capacity** (`heat-capacity`): Extensive equilibrium heat capacities of a fixed amount and composition, with C_V and C_p for a simple compressible system. Excludes: Specific heat capacity is per mass and molar heat capacity is per mole; neither is an alias. No temperature-independent heat capacity or universal finite-change Q=CΔT is assumed. [Reference](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/SPRING/propulsion/notes/node17.html).
- **Isothermal Process** (`isothermal-process`): The constant-temperature constraint, with a separate displayed special case for reversible ideal-gas boundary work. Excludes: Not equivalent to adiabatic or reversible. Constant temperature does not imply constant internal energy for an arbitrary substance. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/3-4-thermodynamic-processes).
- **Adiabatic Process** (`adiabatic-process`): Thermal isolation as a process condition. The displayed energy balance specializes it to a closed system. Excludes: Not synonymous with isentropic or reversible. The relation pV^γ=constant needs a reversible ideal-gas process and constant heat capacities; it is not the general adiabatic condition. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/3-4-thermodynamic-processes).
- **Isobaric Process** (`isobaric-process`): The constant-system-pressure constraint; the work formula is for quasi-static mechanical balance. Excludes: Constant external pressure during an irreversible transient does not ensure constant system pressure. Isobaric does not mean isothermal. [Reference](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node11.html).
- **Isochoric Process** (`isochoric-process`): The fixed-volume constraint for a closed system, with heat-only energy accounting as an explicitly restricted case. Excludes: Does not exclude electrical or shaft work. The equality ΔU=Q requires absence of other work and unchanged bulk kinetic and external potential energies. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/3-5-heat-capacities-of-an-ideal-gas).
- **Heat Engine** (`heat-engine`): The cyclic two-reservoir heat-engine model with heat and work exchanges and no net stored-energy change over one cycle. Excludes: Not the thermodynamic cycle itself. Engines with mass or fuel flows need the corresponding open-system energy balance. [Reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/4-2-heat-engines).
- **Carnot Cycle** (`carnot-cycle`): The reversible two-temperature cycle and its heat-engine efficiency; the working substance need not be an ideal gas. Excludes: Carnot engine is a device implementing the cycle, not an alias. Finite-power operation and irreversible cycles do not attain the reversible efficiency under the same reservoir conditions. [Reference](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node24.html).
