# Broad fields and shared foundations — source review

Reviewed 2026-09-05. These sources support definitions and direct relations, not a unique universal hierarchy or a complete curriculum. Wikipedia links are reading destinations maintained by Illuminatio, not Core identity keys.

## Placement and scope decisions

- Classical mechanics and classical field theory overlap (continuum models); their separate learning entrances do not assert disjoint physics.
- SR and GR are sibling browsing topics under Relativity. The existing GR generalizes SR relation retains its qualified physical meaning, not a navigation-parent command.
- Geodesics and Christoffel symbols are shared differential geometry used in GR. Christoffel symbols are coordinate connection coefficients, not a tensor; null geodesics cannot use proper time as an affine parameter.
- QM and QFT retain separate entrances; QFT includes many-body as well as relativistic applications. The SR prerequisite is explicitly scoped to the introductory relativistic route.
- Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics are distinct, related theories. Boltzmann and thermodynamic entropy are related but not unconditionally identical definitions.
- Group representations and Lie structures remain mathematical objects; no blanket claim that representations decompose or quantum symmetries are all ordinary linear representations.
- Probability distributions need not have densities; expectations and variances retain finite-moment qualifications.
- Mathematical Methods is a legitimate supporting discipline, not a synonym for all Mathematical Physics.

## Primary sources

- [Carroll: Curvature and Gravitation](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll3.html) — The Levi-Civita connection and geodesic equation provide the geometric language used in general relativity; supports the added Christoffel-symbol application link.

- [Carroll: Gravitation](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll4.html) — Freely falling test particles follow spacetime geodesics in the idealized point-particle limit; supports the geodesic application link.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Classical Dynamics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/dt281/dynamics.html) — Curriculum separates finite-dimensional Newtonian, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics from QFT's field-theory preparation. Separate navigation categories are editorial, not a claim that classical mechanics and field theory are disjoint.

- [Cambridge, Tong: The Hamiltonian Formalism](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/dynamics/four.pdf) — Hamiltonian mechanics and classical phase-space generators. The existing hamiltonian ID remains classical; no alias is transferred to the new quantum operator.

- [Cambridge, Tong: QFT Introduction](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S0.html) — QFT applies to relativistic systems and nonrelativistic many-body physics. The SR prerequisite is explicitly scoped to this introductory relativistic branch. Search index supplied readable text while direct Cambridge fetches were intermittent.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Classical Field Theory](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S1.html) — Fields versus finite-dimensional generalized coordinates; field actions, local densities, Noether currents and Hamiltonian field theory. Used for broad field definitions and conservation links.

- [MIT 8.323, Recitation 2: Quantization in QFT](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-323-relativistic-quantum-field-theory-i-spring-2023/mit8_323_s23_rec02.pdf) — Classical scalar transformation law, first-order Lagrangian densities, field Euler-Lagrange equations and canonical quantization. Stationarity is used rather than asserting a minimum.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Free Fields](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S2.html) — Canonical quantization, operator-valued distributions and smearing, mode ladder operators, Fock-space scope and propagators. Canonical quantization is a prescription, not a unique global map of all observables.

- [Debray: Topics in Mathematical Physics, §16](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~adebray/lecture_notes/m393c_notes.pdf) — Hilbert direct sums of symmetric and antisymmetric particle sectors define bosonic and fermionic Fock spaces.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Interacting Fields](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S3.html) — Feynman diagrams encode perturbative terms and associated integrals. Their lines are not asserted to be literal particle paths.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Quantum Electrodynamics, §6.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S6.html) — Gauge equivalence is redundancy, distinguished from global transformations; asymptotic and boundary conditions affect the distinction and possible charges.

- [Carroll: Special Relativity and Flat Spacetime](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March01/Carroll3/Carroll1.html) — Electric and magnetic fields combine as an antisymmetric rank-two tensor; covariant Maxwell equations. Supports cross-links between field theory, relativity and differential forms.

- [Cambridge, Tong: Introduction to General Relativity](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S0.html) — General relativity as a gravitational theory compatible with special relativity. The umbrella node and child memberships remain editorial organization.

- [MIT 8.05, Zwiebach: Wave Mechanics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/61bc31b8d8bf0680c322733910a71aa0_MIT8_05F13_Chap_01.pdf) — The Hamiltonian operator generates Schrödinger evolution; an operator is distinct from the classical Hamiltonian function.

- [MIT 8.321, Lecture 11: Path Integrals](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-321-quantum-theory-i-fall-2017/ea27e6d1653b49990d3d9d317d570328_MIT8_321F17_lec11.pdf) — Time-sliced path integrals, propagators and action weights. Definitions or regulators are required rather than assuming a naive measure over all histories.

- [MIT 18.238: Geometry and Quantum Field Theory](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-238-geometry-and-quantum-field-theory-spring-2023/mit18_238_s23_lec_full.pdf) — Path integrals and their perturbative interpretation; functional integrals are not automatically ordinary rigorously defined real-time measures.

- [Pavel Etingof, Groups Around Us (MIT)](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/groups.pdf) — Lectures 1–3: groups, subgroups, homomorphisms, and symmetry. Supports the group foundations; no implication that every group is finite or a matrix group.

- [Alexander Kirillov Jr., An Introduction to Lie Groups and Lie Algebras](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~kirillov/liegroups/liegroups.pdf) — Definitions 2.1, 3.17, 4.1, and 4.16: Lie groups, Lie algebras, representations and irreducibility. The real/complex Lie-algebra scope avoids characteristic-two conventions. Irreducible does not mean merely indecomposable; representations need not all split.

- [David Skinner, Principles of Quantum Mechanics (Cambridge)](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/dbs26/PQM.html) — Transformations, symmetries and angular momentum support the group-theory connections to quantum mechanics. Quantum symmetry can require projective or antiunitary representations; the linear-group record does not claim to exhaust them.

- [Cambridge Cavendish, Mathematical Methods course](https://www-teach.phy.cam.ac.uk/students/courses/mathematical-methods/79) — A primary curriculum for supporting mathematical methods in physics, including differential and integral methods, variational principles and quantum equations. Mathematical Methods is a broad subject, not a newly invented physical law or a synonym for all mathematical physics.

- [MIT Unified Engineering, Heat, Work and Thermal Equilibrium](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node12.html) — Thermal contact, temperature and the zeroth-law framework; thermal equilibrium is not a claim that all other equilibrium conditions hold.

- [MIT Unified Engineering, First Law of Thermodynamics](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/SPRING/thermodynamics/notes/node14.html) — Energy accounting distinguishes internal and bulk energies. The simple ΔU = Q − W description explicitly fixes bulk kinetic/potential contributions and uses work done by the system.

- [MIT Unified Engineering, Concept and Statements of the Second Law](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node37.html) — Entropy as an equilibrium state function, reversible heat and isolated-system entropy increase. Thermodynamics is kept independent from statistical mechanics as a macroscopic theory.

- [David Tong, Fundamentals of Statistical Mechanics (Cambridge)](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S1.html) — Ensembles, canonical probabilities, partition function and constrained entropy maximization. Supports canonical normalization and its thermodynamic derivatives, and the Lagrange-multiplier application.

- [Julien Tailleur, Equilibrium Statistical Mechanics, Chapter 3 (MIT)](https://www.mit.edu/~jgt/Content-Web/Teaching/8333/Chapter-3.pdf) — Sections 3.1–3.2: microcanonical weighting, microscopic/macroscopic descriptions, continuous phase-space normalization and canonical construction. No unconditional equivalence of ensembles is asserted.

- [TU Delft NB2220, Boltzmann Statistics and Entropy](https://interactivetextbooks.tudelft.nl/NB2220/content/Exercise_set_4.html) — Boltzmann multiplicities, temperature and equilibrium. Boltzmann entropy and thermodynamic entropy have related but explicitly distinct scopes.

- [UC Berkeley Stat 200A — Random Variables and Distributions (A. Adhikari)](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ani/s200af18/Lec_08_28.html) — Defines random variables as functions on an outcome space, distributions, discrete probability masses, continuous densities and cumulative distribution functions. Not all laws have a probability density. The proposed child groups are view-only organization, not subtype assertions.

- [MIT 18.05 — Probability Reading (Jeremy Orloff and Jonathan Bloom)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/mit18_05_s22_probability.pdf) — Primary university introduction to axiomatic probability, random variables, distributions, expectation and variance; supports probability as shared mathematical foundations.

- [MIT 18.175 — Lecture 5: More Integration and Expectation (Scott Sheffield)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-175-theory-of-probability-spring-2014/1c016821763783c34c48816b1ac66969_MIT18_175S14_Lecture5.pdf) — Pages 7–8 define expectation as integration against a probability measure and variance as E[(X−E[X])²]. The descriptions retain integrability and finite-second-moment qualifications; an expectation is not automatically a time average or a sample mean.

- [Cambridge — David Tong, Statistical Physics, Section 1: The Fundamentals of Statistical Mechanics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S1.html) — Describes ensemble probabilities, expectations of observables, canonical mean energy, and energy fluctuations as a variance. Classical observable functions are used for the random-variable application note; quantum ensembles can require density operators, so no unrestricted distribution generalizes ensemble subtype claim is added.
