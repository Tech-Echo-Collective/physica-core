# Electromagnetism foundations — 0.11.0

Twelve additions reviewed on 2026-09-13. The first nine are classical physics; Vector Field, Divergence and Curl are shared mathematics. All previous records are unchanged.

The corresponding Illuminatio editorial pilot reads live Wikipedia page identities and uses independent sources to review definitions and relations. It does not copy article bodies or infer prerequisites from hyperlinks.

- [David Tong, Electromagnetism: introduction and electrostatics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/em/el1.pdf): sections 1.1, 1.2, 2.1.1 and 2.2 support charge, current, E/B fields, Coulomb and Lorentz forces, and electrostatic potential.
- [Feynman Lectures II, chapter 2](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_02.html): sections 2–2 and 2–5 support elementary vector fields, divergence, curl and the differential Maxwell system.
- [Tong, Electrodynamics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/em/em3.pdf): sections 4.3–4.3.1 derive the source-free wave equations and retain the original Maxwell constraints.

Scope decisions: B is not H. Electric current is a signed surface-crossing rate, not current density. Electric potential here is electrostatic. Maxwell equations use total charge/current densities in microscopic SI form. The wave equation is the source-free vacuum case. Constants are symbolic; old exact numerical SI conventions are not adopted.

Learning dependencies are editorial judgments based on the formulas' actual ingredients, not source-authored rankings. Introductory E/B do not require differential forms. Maxwell's component laws are not modeled as its subtypes. The wave equation is derived from Maxwell's system; its mathematical formulation also requires partial differential equations.
