# Transform methods: scope and relation review

Reviewed 2026-09-05 for Core 0.8.0. Four additions follow Illuminatio's scoped Wikipedia review; hyperlinks are discovery hints, not relation evidence. All existing records are preserved.

## Classical methods

[David Tong, Classical Dynamics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/dynamics/clas.pdf), §§4.2, 4.4 and 4.7, supports canonical bracket preservation, phase-space volume and Hamilton–Jacobi theory. Canonical transformations require Poisson brackets in this introductory route. Their symplectic Jacobian preserves Liouville volume, so classical ensemble integrals are invariant under canonical coordinate changes; this statistical-mechanics edge is a mathematical consequence, not a claim that canonical transformations are an ensemble type. Generating functions supply a canonical-transformation route to Hamilton–Jacobi.

[MIT 8.09, Chapter 4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-09-classical-mechanics-iii-fall-2014/f00f7f68ac7ba346a0868efb7430582c_MIT8_09F14_Chapter_4.pdf), §§4.1 and 4.4, treats time-dependent transformations, Hamilton–Jacobi (equations 4.104–4.105) and its semiclassical connection (4.112–4.115). Hamilton–Jacobi requires the Hamiltonian and PDE language; its action relation refers to endpoint variation along stationary trajectories. It is not exact quantum time evolution. No global smooth principal function is assumed.

## Fourier transform

[MIT 6.003, Lecture 16](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-003-signals-and-systems-fall-2011/d74bab2dfa6e465d61fed45763d50528_MIT6_003F11_lec16.pdf), slide 8, gives the continuous transform pair. [Semyon Dyatlov, MIT 18.155](https://math.mit.edu/~dyatlov/18.155/155-notes.pdf), §11.1 (Definition 11.1, Theorem 11.15), supplies a rigorous Schwartz-function inversion setting and the differential-operator correspondence. Calculus is the introductory prerequisite, not a claim that a bounded-interval Riemann integral covers every generalized transform.

[MIT 8.04, Lecture 3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2013/19862abcdf8e4f7cf1321d060cd6dd7c_MIT8_04S13_Lec03.pdf), p. 5, connects wavefunctions in position and momentum representations. [Silvia Nagy, Advanced Quantum Theory](https://www.maths.dur.ac.uk/users/silvia.nagy/QFT.html), §§4.4 and 6.2, uses Fourier transforms of propagators. That application needs the appropriate distributional prescription, not a blanket Schwartz-function assumption for propagators.

## Partial differential equation

[CLP-3, §2.8](https://clp.math.uky.edu/clp3/sec_pde.html) defines PDEs through multivariable functions and partial derivatives and discusses electromagnetic, Einstein and Schrödinger applications. PDEs are not a child of ODEs. The Schrödinger application is limited to position-representation evolution with a local differential Hamiltonian; the existing abstract Hilbert-space equation also covers finite-dimensional systems. Do not infer an unconditional PDE prerequisite or subtype edge for that node.

The schematic finite-order PDE formula belongs to the consumer's display annotations. It does not assert existence, uniqueness or a universal physical law. Navigation depth and shared placements are not semantic `generalizes` edges.
